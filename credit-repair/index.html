Free guide · Credit repair

Credit repair, the honest way — dispute what's wrong, free.

You have seen the reel: "It's over for the credit bureaus — they got sued, so pull all three reports and dispute everything, and look how much money you can make." Part of that is true — duplicate collections and errors are a real, winnable dispute, and it is free. The rest is oversold: you cannot legally erase accurate debts, "disputing everything" can backfire, and credit repair is not a money-making scheme. The five things actually worth disputing

Credit repair is not magic — it is finding what is wrong on your report and making the bureau prove it or remove it. These are the errors that show up most, and that you can win.

1. Accounts that aren't yours

mixed files · identity theft

An account, address, or inquiry you do not recognize — often another person's file mixed into yours, or fraud. These come off when the bureau cannot tie them to you.

2. Duplicates

the reel's real point

The same collection or charge-off listed twice (once by the original creditor, again by a debt buyer). A debt can be reported once — duplicates are a clean, winnable dispute.

3. Wrong balance, status, or dates

paid but still "owed"

A balance you already paid still showing owed, a closed account marked open, a wrong "date of last activity." Inaccurate details must be corrected.

4. Re-aged or expired debt

the 7-year clock

Most negatives must drop off after about seven years. A collector who resets the date to keep it on longer ("re-aging") is violating the law — dispute it.

The fifth: anything the bureau cannot verify. Under the FCRA, if a furnisher does not verify a disputed item within the investigation window, the bureau must correct or delete it. That is the real mechanism the reel is talking about — it works for errors, not for accurate debts you simply want gone.

The honest steps — do it yourself, free

No upfront fee, no "credit repair company." This is the exact order.

  1. Step 1 · Get the factsPull all three reports — free and official. Get your Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion reports free at annualcreditreport.com (the real, government-authorized site — not a look-alike that charges you). Read every line on all three.
  2. Step 2 · Mark what's wrongList only the genuine errors. Circle the accounts that aren't yours, the duplicates, the wrong balances/dates, and the re-aged debt. Do not dispute accurate items — that is what gets a whole batch tossed as "frivolous."
  3. Step 3 · Dispute the bureauFile the dispute with each bureau — in writing. Dispute online or by mail with Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, one item at a time, with a short reason and any proof. They generally must investigate within about 30 days and fix or delete what can't be verified.
  4. Step 4 · Dispute the creditorTell the furnisher too. Dispute directly with the company that reported it (the "furnisher"). They have their own duty to investigate, and hitting both sides is what makes an error actually come off — and stay off.
  5. Step 5 · Escalate a shamFile a free CFPB complaint. If a bureau rubber-stamps "verified" without a real investigation, file a complaint with the CFPB (consumerfinance.gov) — free. This is the leverage the reel is really pointing at, and it works.
  6. Ongoing · Build, don't just deleteAdd positive credit. Repair is half the job. On-time payments, low balances (under ~30% of your limit), and a secured card or credit-builder loan raise a real score. Deleting an error helps; paying on time is what actually rebuilds you.
What's true in the reel — and what isn't. True: you can dispute inaccurate or duplicate items for free, and the CFPB does hold bureaus accountable. Oversold: "the bureaus got sued so everything comes off," "dispute everything," "you can make so much money off this," and "just sue them." You cannot legally remove accurate debts, and credit repair is a fix, not a business model. Treat "make money off this" like any hype line — the real win is a clean report and a higher score, not a payout.

Red flags — the credit-repair scams

The dispute process is legitimate. The companies circling it are where people get robbed. If you hear any of these, walk away.

"Pay us first, then we fix it."Charging before performing services is illegal under the Credit Repair Organizations Act. Up-front fee = keep your money.
"Guaranteed deletion — even accurate items."No one can legally remove accurate, verifiable information. A "guarantee" is a lie.
"We'll build you a new credit identity / CPN."A "CPN" used in place of your SSN is fraud. It can be a federal crime — never do this.
"Dispute everything, even what's real."Bulk disputing accurate items gets them flagged as frivolous and can freeze your real disputes.
"Don't contact the bureaus yourself."The law gives you the right to dispute for free. Anyone telling you not to is protecting their fee.
"Sue them and get rich."Litigation is a real attorney's last resort with real requirements — not a prompt-and-payday scheme.

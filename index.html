When a property stops being an asset

Payments fall behind. A house is inherited from three states away. A rental sits empty. The repairs cost more than the owner wants to take on. In every one of those situations the worst outcome is the same: doing nothing until the decision gets made for you.

Behind on payments

Virginia foreclosures are non-judicial. That means no judge and no court case is needed: a trustee can set a sale date quickly and sell the property on the courthouse steps.

At that auction the owner usually walks away with nothing. A sale arranged before the sale date can leave money in the owner's pocket instead.

A trustee can also postpone or cancel a sale at any time, so a date on a notice is not always the final date.

Inherited, vacant, or a rental you are done with

Estates, out-of-state heirs, houses that have sat empty for years, and landlords who have had enough of the calls.

Sold as-is. No clearing it out first, no repairs on your side, and no need to fly in to get it ready.

Needs more work than it is worth

Fire or water damage, a failed system, code violations, or a renovation that stopped halfway.

We look at properties in as-is condition, so the owner is not spending money on repairs just to be able to sell.

How it works

  1. Tell us about the property. Email us the address and whatever you know: the situation, whether anyone is living there, what shape it is in, and whether there is a sale date. You do not need paperwork ready to start the conversation.
  2. You get a straight written answer within days. We look at the property and the numbers and come back to you in writing with what we can do, or with an honest no. If a different route would serve you better, we say that instead of wasting your time.
  3. Close on your date. No repairs, no commissions, and no fees charged to the seller. You pick the closing date, and if you need more time in the house after closing we can talk about that too.

Owed money after a tax sale?

In Florida, when a property is sold at a tax-deed sale for more than what was owed, the difference does not disappear. The county may hold that surplus money in the former owner's name, and a great many people never learn it is there.

We search for those surplus funds and help the person entitled to them make the claim.

  • Nothing up front. You pay nothing to have us look, and nothing out of pocket to start a claim.
  • The fee is capped by Florida law. Florida Statutes section 45.033 caps what a recovery agent may charge at 12 percent of the surplus.
  • The money is paid to the claimant. The county pays the person entitled to the funds, not the person who found them.

You are always free to file a surplus claim yourself, directly with the county, at no cost to you.

01 — How we rank

A prop firm funds you with its own capital after you pass a paid evaluation, then splits the profit with you. Done right, it's the cheapest way to trade real size. But 2026 isn't 2022. After the 2024 shakeout — when roughly one in seven firms shut down, some still owing traders money — and after MetaQuotes pulled MT4/MT5 from prop firms serving US clients, the field is smaller and the rules matter more than the price. So we rank on payout reliability, rule stability, and US access — not the cheapest challenge.

04 — Questions, answered

What exactly is a prop firm?
A proprietary-trading firm lets you trade the firm's capital instead of your own. Pass a paid evaluation (hit a profit target while respecting risk limits), get a funded account, keep the majority profit split (commonly 80–90%). You're not risking your own money in the market — but the evaluation fee is a real, at-risk cost.
Are prop firms legit, or a scam?
The model is legitimate and the established firms have paid out serious money. But the industry is uneven — some firms shut down, changed rules mid-challenge, or delayed payouts. That's why we rank on payout reliability and rule stability, and give every firm an honest con. Treat any evaluation fee as money you could lose.
Can US traders use these firms?
It varies and it changes — some restrict US clients or certain platforms/products due to regulation and data-feed rules. We flag US availability per firm, but always confirm on the firm's own site at signup.
What does it cost, and do I keep my profits?
Evaluation fees scale with account size (tens up to a few hundred dollars for common sizes — check current pricing). On a funded account you keep a majority split (commonly 80–90%, sometimes higher with milestones). Most traders don't pass first try, so budget for more than one attempt.

The "free leads, no license" pitch vs. what really happens

The reel's promise

  • "$20,000 a month signing up seniors — from your phone."
  • "No license, no experience — free leads handed to you."
  • "Charge a small fee to help them claim their benefits."
  • "There's a $2,900 flex card the government owes every senior."
  • "Buy my $997 course and I'll give you the whole system."

The honest reality

  • Selling Medicare or insurance requires a state license and, for Medicare, AHIP certification every year.
  • "Free leads" is you buying leads or feeding an MLM downline — that is the product being sold to you.
  • You are never paid by the senior — the carrier pays you a commission capped by Medicare (CMS).
  • Medicare, Medicaid, SSI and Social Security are free to claim. Charging a fee for that is often illegal.
  • The "$2,900 flex card" cold call is a scam — the government never calls asking for your Medicare number.

$0
is what you can legally charge a senior to enroll them in Medicare, Medicaid, SSI or Social Security — those benefits are free to apply for, through the .gov sites and free SHIP counselors. The money in this business is real, but it comes from carrier commissions a licensed agent earns, never from a fee taken out of a senior's pocket.

Where the real money actually is — four honest lanes

Helping seniors get the right coverage is a genuinely good business. The income is real, steady and repeatable — it comes from being licensed, honest and easy to trust, not from a signup fee. Here is where the money honestly comes from.

1. Medicare plans

commission, paid by the carrier

A licensed agent helps a senior choose a Medicare Advantage or Supplement plan that fits their doctors and medicines. The insurance carrier pays the agent a commission at a rate Medicare itself caps — the senior pays the agent nothing. Do it honestly and the same clients stay with you for years.

2. Life & final expense

the second, steady line

Many seniors also want a small final-expense or whole-life policy so a funeral never lands on their family. A licensed life agent is paid a commission by the insurer. It is real protection people genuinely need — sold plainly, without pressure or scare tactics.

3. Referral partnerships

the repeat-work engine

Steady clients come from people who trust you: senior communities, hospital discharge planners, financial advisors and even SHIP volunteers. They send you seniors who need honest help. Be licensed, compliant and kind once, and those partners feed you referrals for years.

4. Renewals & annual review

income that compounds

Every year a client keeps a plan, the carrier pays a renewal commission — and the yearly enrollment period is when you review their coverage and help them switch if a better plan appeared. A book of happy, well-served seniors is the real "$20k a month," built slowly and honestly.

Put simply: the money is in commissions, renewals and referrals — all paid by carriers, all earned by service. "Charge the senior a fee" is not a lane; it is the thing that gets your license pulled and can start a fraud case.

The honest path — build a real senior-benefits business

This is the real order of operations to turn helping seniors into a business families trust and partners refer to. No "charge a fee" or "free money" shortcut required — that shortcut is the trap.

  1. Step 0 · Register the businessBe a real, findable agency. Set up an LLC and EIN so the agency is separate from you and looks legitimate to carriers and referral partners. A registered entity is what a carrier, a senior community and a compliance reviewer all check first.
  2. Step 1 · Get licensed & appointedLicense, AHIP, E&O, appointments. Pass your state's health and/or life insurance exam, carry errors & omissions (E&O) insurance, get appointed by each carrier you want to represent, and — for anything Medicare — complete AHIP certification every year. This is non-negotiable: selling without it is illegal and uninsured.
  3. Step 2 · Learn the marketing rulesCMS compliance is the whole game. Medicare marketing is tightly regulated: no cold-calling Medicare beneficiaries, a signed Scope of Appointment before you discuss plans, no misleading "government" branding, and accurate materials only. Learn the current CMS rules and your state's — breaking them ends the business fast.
  4. Step 3 · Get clients honestlyBuild the referral engine. Introduce yourself to senior communities, discharge planners, financial advisors and local SHIP offices. Host free, compliant educational events. List the agency on Google with real reviews. One well-served senior who tells their friends is worth more than any purchased lead.
  5. Step 4 · Serve them rightEnroll, review, protect. Match each senior to the plan that fits their doctors, drugs and budget — not the one that pays you most. Review it every year, and guard their Medicare number and personal data like your own. The agent who protects a senior is the agent that senior's whole family calls next.
  6. Step 5 · Grow itTeam, systems, retention. Add licensed agents, standardize your compliant process, and track renewals. Layer in life and final-expense for clients who want it. Honest service compounds into a renewal book — that is the real "$20k a month," earned the way that lasts and keeps your license clean.
What "charge a fee" and "free money you're owed" really are: a scam — and often a crime. Medicare, Medicaid, SSI and Social Security are free to apply for through medicare.gov, ssa.gov and free SHIP counselors, and charging a senior to enroll them is against the rules. Nobody who calls, texts or DMs about a "$2,900 flex card" or "money the government owes you" is the government — the real agencies do not cold-call for your Medicare number or bank details. A licensed agent is paid by the carrier, never by the senior.

Red flags — for the agent and the senior

The business is real. The reels and the shady "agencies" around it are where people get scammed or lose their license. If you hear any of these — whether you are chasing the reel or a senior being pitched — walk away.

"$20k a month, free leads, no license."You cannot legally sell insurance without a state license. "Free leads" means you are the customer — buying leads or joining a downline.
"No license or certification needed."Every Medicare sale requires a license plus AHIP certification renewed yearly. Anyone skipping it is selling illegally.
"Charge the senior a fee to claim their benefits."Medicare, Medicaid, SSI and Social Security are free. Charging to enroll is often illegal — a licensed agent is paid by the carrier.
"Buy my $997 course to learn the secret."The real steps — license, AHIP, E&O, compliance, referrals — are free and on this page. The course is the actual product being sold.
(Senior side) "The government called about your $2,900 flex card."The government never cold-calls for your Medicare number or bank info. Hang up. Real help is free at 1-800-MEDICARE and medicare.gov.
(Senior side) "Give me your Medicare number to claim it."Guard your Medicare number like a credit card. Free, unbiased help is a SHIP counselor — not a caller who found you first.

How much should a small business website cost?

Luxucleen LLC · updated September 2026 · ~4 min read

For most small businesses, a simple website should cost a few hundred dollars, one time — not thousands, and not a monthly bill forever. Here is what the different options really cost, the fees nobody mentions, and what you actually need.

What each option really costs

  • An agency: $2,000 to $10,000+ for a small-business site, often with a monthly retainer on top. Great work — overkill for a one-location business.
  • A freelancer: $500 to $3,000, and quality swings a lot. It can also drag on for weeks.
  • A DIY builder (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy): looks "free" but really $15 to $40 every month, forever — plus many hours of your own time, and it is easy to end up with something slow that does not work well on a phone.
  • A simple pro one-pager: a few hundred dollars, one time, done for you in days.

What drives the price up

  • Number of pages and custom design.
  • Online store / bookings (real e-commerce costs more — and most small businesses do not need it to start).
  • Ongoing maintenance — the monthly fee that quietly adds up to more than the site itself.

What a small business actually needs

Most customers find you on their phone. What turns that search into a call is simple: who you are, what you do, your hours and area, a few real photos, and a big call, text or directions button. A fast one-page site that does exactly that beats a slow ten-page site every time — and it is far cheaper.

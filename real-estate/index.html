Sell before the auction

Virginia foreclosures are non-judicial. That means no judge and no court case is needed: a trustee can set a sale date quickly and sell the property on the courthouse steps, and those dates move fast. At that auction the owner usually walks away with nothing. A sale arranged before the sale date — to anyone, not necessarily to us — can leave money in the owner's pocket instead.

A trustee can also postpone or cancel a sale at any time, so a date on a notice is not always the final date. If you have a notice in your hand, send it over and we will read it with you.

Behind on payments, but you want to keep more of your equity

If your loan is a government-backed one — VA, FHA or USDA — it may be assumable. That means a buyer can take over your existing loan and its interest rate instead of getting a new one at today's rate. When the rate on that loan is low, being able to take it over is worth real money to a buyer, and that can mean a higher price for you than an as-is cash sale would bring.

It is not automatic: the lender has to approve the buyer, the buyer has to cover the difference between the price and the loan balance, and it takes longer than a cash close. With a VA loan there is a further catch worth knowing — unless the buyer is a veteran who substitutes their own entitlement, yours can stay tied to that house. We check whether your loan qualifies and tell you which route actually leaves you with more, including when that route is not us.

Money the county may already owe you

In Florida, when a property is sold at a tax-deed sale for more than what was owed, the difference does not disappear. The county may hold that surplus money in the former owner's name, and a great many people never learn it is there.

We search for those surplus funds and help the person entitled to them make the claim.

  • Nothing up front. You pay nothing to have us look, and nothing out of pocket to start a claim.
  • The fee is capped by Florida law. Florida Statutes section 45.033 caps what a recovery agent may charge at 12 percent of the surplus.
  • The money is paid to the claimant. The county pays the person entitled to the funds, not the person who found them.

You are always free to file a surplus claim yourself, directly with the county, at no cost to you. Our free education page explains how to do exactly that, whether or not you ever use us.
